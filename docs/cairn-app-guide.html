The problem Cairn is built to solve

If you've ever thought: "I know the rule. I broke the rule. I lost. I promised to follow the rule. I broke it again" — that loop is a discipline-and-process problem, not a knowledge gap. Roughly 80% discipline, 20% knowledge. You already know how to take a good trade. What's missing is a system that prevents the bad ones. Cairn is that system — an external discipline layer that replaces willpower with structure.

1

Prevent, in real time

Rule violations get blocked before the click, not detected afterwards in a report you'll skim once and forget.

2

Capture honest data

Every trade records the complete, honest truth — with no escape hatch for laziness or tilt. Fields that need self-awareness can't be skipped or defaulted away.

3

Change behavior weekly

That data becomes insight that actually shifts what you do next week — not a vanity dashboard you check once a month.

What Cairn is not

Not a charting tool

You trade on TradingView and MT5/cTrader. Cairn is the discipline layer around your existing setup, not a chart replacement.

Not a social platform

No feeds, no sharing, no leaderboard comparisons. This is between you and your own rules.

Not a coach

Cairn enforces your own pre-committed rules. It gives no trading advice, no signals, no opinions on your setups.

Not tied to any prop firm

Prop firms are configured as generic, user-named "firms" with your own rule sets — nothing is hardcoded to any specific company.

The voice

Cairn talks like a respected mentor: direct, calm, honest. Never cutesy, never alarmist, never patronizing. It's a small detail that shows up everywhere in the app.

Trading through Cairn

New Trade — the Pre-Trade Gate FREE

A slide-over panel that is the heart of "prevention over detection." It auto-fills context (account, phase, session bias, trade count today, current daily P&L, active cooldowns), then walks you through pair, setup, killzone (auto-detected from the clock, overridable), direction, and your three prices — entry, stop, target. As you type, it live-computes SL/TP in pips, your risk:reward ratio (color-coded red/amber/green), your lot size from your risk %, and your dollar risk. One-tap confluence checkboxes (MSS confirmed, HTF bias aligned, DXY aligned, SMT confirmed) capture your reasoning. A required invalidation field ("this trade is wrong if…", minimum 20 characters) forces you to state your out before you're in. Three emotion sliders (calm / urgency / need-this-to-work) capture your state of mind. At the bottom, a live rule-check panel shows every relevant rule as ✓ pass / ⚠ warning / ✗ block — if anything blocks, the submit button is disabled and red. If everything's clear, it's "Ready to Trade." Confirming asks one honest question: "Order placed in broker?" — only then does the session lock and the trade go live.

Trade Modification FREE

While a trade is open, you can move your SL/TP, close manually, or log a partial close — all from the trade detail view. Try to widen your stop against the position and the no_sl_widening rule fires immediately. Every modification is permanently logged.

Post-Trade Log (Close Trade) FREE

Exit price/time/reason (TP / SL / Manual / BE / Timeout / Partial-then-full), optional MAE/MFE in pips, and auto-computed P&L in $, R, and %, plus duration. A rules-broken checklist lets you tick anything the engine didn't already auto-detect. A short post-trade reflection (calm score, one sentence on what you did right, one on what you did wrong, free tags) and up to 4 optional screenshots close the loop. None of the honesty fields default to "clean" — you either answer them or the trade is explicitly marked unreviewed.

Trade Log & Trade Detail FREE

A full, filterable history — by date, account, pair, setup, killzone, mode (live/sim/backtest), clean vs. dirty, win/loss/BE, and tags — with a fully sortable, user-configurable column set. Click into any trade for the complete record: every field, a screenshot gallery, exactly which rules were evaluated at submit time, related trades from the same day, and export/duplicate/delete actions. Bulk-export any selection to CSV.

Core System

Accounts & Phases

In Cairn, an account is one prop-firm challenge attempt (or your own live/personal account) — its own balance, its own drawdown rules, its own rule configuration, its own trade history. You can run as many accounts side by side as you actually have.

Prop firms — generic, never hardcoded

Cairn never mentions a specific prop firm by name anywhere in the interface. Instead, you create your own "firm" entries (or use the built-in "Custom" one) and name them however you like. A firm just carries a default step count — 1, 2, or 3 — that new account templates for it default to.

Account templates

A template is a reusable blueprint so you don't re-enter the same numbers for every new challenge. Build one once, spin up new accounts from it in seconds. A template captures every one of these fields:

FieldWhat it controls
Step count1, 2, or 3-step challenge structure.
Account sizeStarting balance for this challenge tier.
Leveragee.g. 30:1, 50:1, 100:1 — used in every lot-size calculation.
Daily drawdown type & valuePercent of balance, percent of equity, or a fixed dollar amount.
Total drawdown type & valueSame three types, for the overall account-wide limit.
Drawdown basisMeasured from initial balance, the high-water mark, or the previous day's close — exactly matches how your firm actually calculates it.
Profit target per phaseSeparate target % for Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 if applicable.
Min / max trading daysSome firms require a minimum number of active trading days, or cap the challenge window.
Weekend holding allowedWhether you're permitted to carry positions into the weekend.
News trading allowedWhether trading through high-impact news is permitted.
Consistency rule %Caps any single day's profit as a percentage of total profit, if your firm enforces one.

Templates can be exported and imported as JSON, so you can back one up or share it between setups.

Phases

Every account tracks a current_phase: 1, 2, 3 if your firm uses a third step, or 0 once you're funded. Cairn shows your phase progress right on the Dashboard and Accounts screen — current equity vs. starting balance, drawdown used vs. available, and profit-target progress, all updating live as you trade.

Account lifecycle

Each account carries a status: active, passed, failed (with a required reason), paused, or retired/archived. The Accounts screen lists every account you've ever run — display name, firm, size, phase, current equity, drawdown used/available, profit-target progress, days alive, trades taken, and clean-trade rate — click any row to switch the whole app's context to that account.

Accounts & Phases analytics

A dedicated analytics tab (see the Analytics page) turns your account history into a ladder: every account chronologically, a rolling Phase 1 / Phase 2 / overall funded pass-rate trend, total challenge cost spent vs. payouts earned, a breakdown of which rule gets broken most often right before a failure, a distribution of how many days accounts typically survive, and auto-generated pattern insights like "70% of your blown accounts broke max_trades_per_day within 48h of failure."

How to use it day to day:

Settings → Account Templates to build your blueprint once. Settings → Prop Firms if you want to label which firm it's for. Accounts → New Account to spin up a real challenge attempt from that template (or fully custom). Everything else — phase tracking, drawdown math, profit-target progress — updates itself as you log trades.

FREE Accounts, templates, and phase tracking are all part of the free, local-forever core of Cairn.
Core System — The Moat

The Rule Engine

This is the single most important system in Cairn, and it's entirely free. Its job is simple to state and hard to build: enforce your own pre-committed rules, in real time, before the damage is done.

Three checkpoints

1. Pre-trade gate

Every rule relevant to a new trade is evaluated live as you fill out the New Trade panel — before you can submit.

2. Trade-modification gate

Try to move a stop loss against your position or add size mid-trade, and the relevant rule fires immediately.

3. Session gate

Account-level checks — daily loss limits, cooldowns, trade counts — evaluated continuously across your whole session.

The full rule catalog

Every rule below is independently enabled, disabled, and configured per account in Settings → Rules. Severity is either blocking (stops the trade cold), warning (lets you proceed but makes you look at it), or logged (recorded silently for analytics).

Every evaluation is logged — passes, warnings, blocks, and overrides all write to rule_violations. That's the raw behavioral dataset the Analytics and Insight Engine pages run on.

The override system

Some rules allow an override — but Cairn makes it deliberately effortful, on purpose. A modal shows exactly which rule is firing and why, the current value vs. the threshold, and requires you to type (not click) the word OVERRIDE plus a written reason of at least 30 characters. The event is logged permanently as outcome: 'user_overrode', and the trade is tagged not clean, forever — no analytic will ever quietly call it a clean trade.

You are moving SL against you. This violates Rule 4 on this account. Proceed anyway?

Cooldowns

When a cooldown triggers, a persistent countdown banner appears and new trades are blocked until it clears — either the timer runs out, or (for consecutive-loss cooldowns) you type the exact phrase "I am trading my plan, not my emotions." There's no quiet dismiss button.

Hard locks

Some limits can never be overridden — the hard daily-drawdown stop is the clearest example. Once triggered, the session enters a locked state: the Dashboard shows it plainly, trade entry is disabled everywhere, and the app tells you exactly which rule fired and when it clears.

Session states

How to customize:

Settings → Rules (per account, with global defaults) — toggle any rule on/off, edit its config value, reset everything to defaults, or export/import your whole rule profile as JSON to reuse across accounts.

FREE The entire rule engine — every rule, override, cooldown, and hard lock — is free, forever. This is Cairn's moat, and it is never paywalled.

Killzones & Customization

A killzone is simply a time window that historically sees higher volume and volatility — the idea being that trading inside these windows gives you a statistical edge over trading in dead, directionless hours. If you're new to this: think of it as "the hours when the market actually tends to move."

Default killzones

Seeded on first launch (all times UTC — displayed converted to your own timezone):

KillzoneWindow (UTC)
Asia00:00 – 05:00
London07:00 – 10:00
NY AM12:00 – 15:00
London Silver Bullet09:00 – 10:00
NY PM Silver Bullet19:00 – 20:00

The New Trade panel auto-detects which killzone you're currently in from the clock (you can override it), and the require_killzone rule can block entries taken outside your configured windows entirely.

Everything is customizable — nothing is a hardcoded constraint

Cairn's design principle: any behavioral preference is a setting, not an assumption. That includes:

ScopeWhat you can change
GlobalPairs, setups, killzones, prop firms, account templates, theme, timezone, week-start day, number formatting.
Per accountEvery single rule — enable/disable and its config value — plus the display name.
Per sessionBias fields and key levels for that day.

Pairs

A seeded instrument list ships out of the box — majors, gold, key indices, and BTC — and you can add, edit, or archive anything. Each pair carries: a unique symbol, a display name, its asset class (forex / indices / commodities / crypto / stocks / other), the pip decimal (how many decimal places equal one pip), the pip value per standard lot, and an optional list of correlated symbols used for SMT divergence checks.

Setups

Seeded with ten ICT/SMC-native setup types: Fair Value Gap (FVG), Order Block, Breaker Block, Mitigation Block, Judas Swing, SMT Divergence, Silver Bullet, Liquidity Sweep Reversal, Turtle Soup, and Power of Three. Each has a name, category, description, and display color for tags and charts — add your own or edit these freely.

Analytics & Insights

The Discipline Ring & Composite Score

The Discipline Ring is the first thing you see on the Dashboard — a large ring showing your live discipline standing. Alongside it sits a Composite Performance Score (a 0–100 rating in the spirit of a "Zella Score," if you've used other journals) built from your win rate, profit factor, average win/loss size, consistency, and clean-trade rate — computed with exact decimal math, never floating-point rounding.

Performance

Per-trade A–F grade

Every trade gets a letter grade, computed at read time — never stored, always fresh. It's process-weighted: whether you followed your plan and kept your rules clean dominates the grade far more than the outcome. A disciplined, well-managed loss can grade an A. A rule-breaking win can grade an F. The grade rewards the behavior, not the luck.

Total trades, win rate, R-expectancy, total R, net P&L, profit factor. An equity curve (toggle $/R) with a max-drawdown overlay, an R-multiple distribution histogram, streak tracking, and a daily P&L heatmap calendar.

Your weekly review form and history, a monthly summary preview, and — see below — the local insight engine's live findings.

The local insight engine

Deterministic statistical heuristics run entirely on your device — no LLM call, no network round-trip, no telemetry, ever. It surfaces things like:

  • "Your win rate drops when urgency scores above 7."
  • "You show negative expectancy trading outside your listed killzones."
  • "Tilt cycle detected: three consecutive losses followed by an over-sized trade, N times in the last M weeks."
  • "Setup X is your highest-expectancy setup, but you only take it N% of the time."

These only surface once there's a statistically meaningful sample — no confident-sounding claim from three trades.

Trust & Ownership

Security, Privacy & Your Data

This is a financial app that touches your trading psychology. The posture is deliberately strict.

Local-first, always

The desktop app is fully usable offline, forever, with or without an account. Your local SQLite database is the canonical store on your machine — it is never a cache of "the real data somewhere else." No telemetry is ever collected by default; any crash reporting is strictly opt-in and defaults to off.

Optional end-to-end-encrypted cloud sync PRO

Pro adds optional multi-device sync where the server is architecturally incapable of reading your content:

  • Data is encrypted on your device, using a data key wrapped by a key derived from your password (Argon2id), before anything leaves your machine.
  • The key that unlocks it all never leaves your device.
  • The server stores only ciphertext and a wrapped key — no admin override, no backdoor, ever.
  • Free users never have any vault data on the server at all.
  • Cancelling Pro stops sync but never deletes your local data.

Money math you can trust

Every monetary and pip calculation uses exact decimal arithmetic, never floating-point numbers — tested with property-based tests, not just example cases. Every database write is transactional, and every migration is tested running both forward and backward.

Backups & your right to leave

Point Cairn's backup system at any folder you like — including one already synced by Dropbox or Google Drive — set a schedule, and restore from any point. Export everything to JSON or CSV at any time, or open your raw local data folder directly in your OS's file explorer. Nothing is ever locked in.

Broker safety boundary

Cairn is read-only against every broker connection, permanently. No adapter, no Expert Advisor, no integration contains any order-placing, order-modifying, or order-closing code path — this is asserted by an automated test, not just a policy statement.

Sub-page · Free Feature

Connect MetaTrader 5 & cTrader

Cairn's live broker bridges auto-log your trades in real time and watch your live position for rule violations — read-only, forever. This page is the full step-by-step; a separate interactive checklist version of the same guide is also available if you'd like a progress-tracked copy.

Two separate things happen when you connect a broker:

(1) The connection — Cairn and your broker start talking. (2) Account mapping — you tell Cairn which of your Cairn accounts a broker account belongs to. Until mapped, fills are held and no trade is created. Cairn never guesses.

Part 0 — Before you start

If this is a fresh Cairn install with no accounts yet, open Cairn → Accounts and create at least one (any name, any starting balance/phase) before continuing — account mapping later needs somewhere to bind to.

Part A — MetaTrader 5 (do this first)

MT5 has no outbound API for third-party apps, so Cairn ships a small, read-only Expert Advisor (EA) that runs inside your MT5 terminal and reports fills to Cairn over a connection that never leaves your computer.

A1 — Open the Cairn screen you need

Cairn → Settings → Integrations → scroll to the MetaTrader 5 bridge card. You'll see a Pairing token and a Listener address like 127.0.0.1:53127.

A2 — Install the EA (one click)

Open any MT5 terminal you have installed, then click Install Cairn EA in Cairn. This installs the bridge into every MT5 terminal Cairn detects on your machine at once — it doesn't matter which one you actually trade from. If the EA ever gets deleted by accident, just click the button again; it's harmless to re-run.

A3 — Compile it (skip if already compiled)

If a CairnBridge.ex5 file is already sitting in the Experts folder, it's pre-compiled — skip this. Otherwise: press F4 in MT5 to open MetaEditor, open CairnBridge.mq5, press F7 to compile (expect 0 errors, 0 warnings), then close MetaEditor.

A4 — ⚠ Allow the local connection

The step people miss. In MT5: Tools → Options → Expert Advisors → tick Allow algorithmic trading → tick the WebRequest/URL-allow option and add exactly 127.0.0.1 → click OK. This master EA switch does not give the bridge trading power — it has no order-placing code at all.

A5 — Attach the EA and paste the token

Open any chart → drag CairnBridge from Navigator → Expert Advisors onto it → in the Inputs tab set InpToken (paste from Cairn), InpPort (match Cairn's listener, default 53127), leave InpHost as 127.0.0.1 → click OK. Look for a 🙂 in the chart's corner.

A6 — Confirm it worked

Back in Cairn's Integrations page, within seconds the MetaTrader 5 status should turn green: "Listening on 127.0.0.1:<port>," and the MT5 panel shows "EA connected" with a ticking "last event" timer.

Login errors are separate.

An MT5 Journal message like authorization failed (Invalid account) is your broker login, unrelated to the bridge. The heartbeat still works even while logged out — you just won't see real fills until you're logged into a valid account.

Part B — cTrader (needs Spotware's approval first)

cTrader has an official, free Open API, but you first register a small "application" with Spotware and wait for their review — typically a few business days. The Cairn side takes two minutes.

  1. Register a free app on Spotware's Open API portal. Redirect URI must be exactly http://127.0.0.1:53129/ctrader/callback, scope accounts (read-only).
  2. Copy the Client ID and Client Secret Spotware gives you.
  3. Paste both into Cairn → Settings → Integrations → cTrader, and click Save credentials (stored in your OS keychain, never in plain text).
  4. Choose Demo or Live, click Connect cTrader, log in and approve read-only access in the browser tab that opens.
  5. Click Test connection to confirm.

If Spotware hasn't finished reviewing your app yet, the connect flow will say so plainly — nothing wrong on your end, just wait it out.

Part C — Account mapping (one-time, per broker account)

Place (or wait for) one trade on the connected broker account — it appears under Waiting to be mapped within seconds. Pick which Cairn account it belongs to, click Bind. That's permanent: you never have to re-map that same broker account again, even across app restarts or EA reinstalls.

What "connected" gets you

Every fill auto-fills the mechanical facts of the trade — pair, direction, lot size, entry/stop/target, exit, partials — and Cairn's rule engine watches your live position, warning you calmly and immediately if it drifts from your own rules: a stop moved against you, size increased mid-trade, a target cut short, an over-trade past your daily limit, trading after a loss-limit lockout, or an entry outside your configured killzones.

What it will never do:

Place, modify, or close a trade for you. Both bridges are permanently read-only. Cairn cannot block a trade that's already live at the broker — it can only detect and warn. That's a hard, deliberate safety boundary, not a current limitation.
