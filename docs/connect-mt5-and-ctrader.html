Connecting Cairn to MetaTrader 5 & cTrader

A step-by-step checklist. Check items off as you go — your progress is saved automatically in this browser.

Two things happen when you connect a broker: (1) the connection — Cairn and your broker start talking, and (2) account mapping — you tell Cairn which of your Cairn accounts a broker account belongs to. Until you map it, fills are held and no trade is created. Cairn never guesses.

Part 0 — Before you start

fresh installs only

If this is a brand-new Cairn install with no accounts yet, account mapping later on has nothing to bind to.

Part A — MetaTrader 5

do this first — no waiting

MT5 has no way for outside apps to talk to it over the internet, so Cairn uses a small helper file called an Expert Advisor (EA) that lives inside your MT5 terminal and reports your fills to Cairn over a connection that never leaves your computer.

A1 — Open the Cairn screen you'll need

A2 — Install the EA into MT5 (one click)

If Cairn didn't detect your terminal: open MT5 first, then click Install Cairn EA again. Still nothing? Do it manually — MT5: File → Open Data Folder → MQL5 → Experts — and copy CairnBridge.mq5 there yourself.
Accidentally deleted CairnBridge? No need to hunt for the file — just click Install Cairn EA in Cairn again. It re-copies both files fresh, every time.

A3 — Compile it inside MT5

Skip this whole step if the Experts folder already shows a CairnBridge.ex5 file — that means it's pre-compiled and ready.

A4 — ⚠️ Allow the local connection (the step people miss)

MT5 blocks any outside connection by default, even a local one. This step is mandatory.

A5 — Attach the EA to a chart and paste the token

A6 — Confirm it worked

A note on login errors: the EA bridge and your MT5 account login are separate things. A Journal message like authorization on MetaQuotes-Demo failed (Invalid account) is a broker-login problem — it does not stop the EA from running or reaching Cairn. The heartbeat will work even while logged out; you just won't see real fills until you're logged into a valid account.

Part B — cTrader

takes longer — Spotware approval needed

Unlike MT5, cTrader has an official API, but you first register a small "application" with cTrader's parent company, Spotware, and wait for their approval. Budget a few business days — the Cairn side takes two minutes, the waiting is on Spotware.

B1 — Register a free application with Spotware

New Spotware apps usually start "pending" and can't complete login until Spotware activates them (KYC/review, typically a few business days). If B3 fails immediately saying the app isn't active, that's why — nothing wrong on your end.

Part C — Account mapping

same for both brokers

The last step, and it's identical for MT5 and cTrader.

From here on, every fill from that broker account automatically creates a Cairn trade — a draft awaiting reflection by default, or a fully logged trade if "When a broker fill arrives" is set to Fully auto-logged. To fix a wrong binding later, find it under "Mapped accounts" and change the dropdown, or click Remove.

What "connected" actually gets you

Once mapped, Cairn will auto-fill the mechanical facts of every trade (pair, direction, lot size, entry/stop/target, exit, partials) — no manual re-entry — and warn you in real time, calmly, if your live position drifts from your own rules: stop moved against you, size increased mid-trade, target cut short, over-trading past your daily limit, trading after a loss-limit lockout, or trading outside your configured session hours.

It will never place, modify, or close a trade for you — both bridges are read-only by design, permanently.

Both bridges are optional. Cairn's manual trade log works exactly the same with or without a broker connected — connecting one just removes the retyping.
