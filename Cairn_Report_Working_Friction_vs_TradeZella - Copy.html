01

How Cairn Works — A User's Walkthrough

Cairn is a private, local desktop app that you trade through, not a journal you fill in afterwards. Its whole reason for existing is to stop rule-breaking before the click, capture honest data on every trade, and turn that data into behaviour change. Here is exactly what you can do, in the order you'd do it on a trading day.

1.1 — What you see when you open the app

Cairn opens to a left sidebar with six places to go. Everything in the app lives under one of these:

📊 Dashboard 📒 Trades 📈 Analytics 🏦 Accounts 📝 Review ⚙️ Settings
DASHBOARD

Your cockpit. A Discipline Ring (a 0–100 score of how cleanly you've been trading), current account balance, today's P&L, win/loss streak, open positions, and a button to log a new trade.

TRADES

The full trade log — every planned, open and closed trade. Drafts show an Activate button; open trades show a Close button; closed trades open a detail view with the full story and screenshots.

ANALYTICS

Six tabs: Performance, Setup Performance, Behavioural, Rule Adherence, Accounts/Phases, and Review — each backed by real SQL over your trades, with a filter bar on top.

ACCOUNTS

Your prop-firm or personal accounts: size, leverage, drawdown limits, profit targets, phase, and the per-account rule set that the engine enforces.

REVIEW

Structured weekly/session reflection + PDF export. (Currently the export works; the structured review screen itself is still a stub — see Part 2.)

SETTINGS

Everything configurable: firms, account templates, pairs, setups, killzones, R-target alerts, backups, timezone, risk defaults, keyboard shortcuts.

1.2 — First launch: onboarding (one time)

Before you can trade, Cairn walks you through a short setup: welcome → pick theme → create a "firm" → choose an account template → create your first account → review the seeded defaults (19 instruments, ICT setups, killzones) → point it at a backup folder → done. After this, you land on the Dashboard.

1.3 — The trading-day lifecycle

Every day flows through three phases. This is the spine of the whole app:

The three phases of a Cairn trading day. Rule enforcement happens in the BEFORE box — that's the whole point.

1.4 — BEFORE: logging your bias and planning the trade

Step A — Session bias (once per day, per account)

You open the Session Bias modal and record your read of the market: Daily, 4H and 1H bias (bullish/bearish/neutral) — each with a written reason — plus optional HTF liquidity target, DXY bias, SMT notes, a session plan and key levels. Once saved this "locks" the session, and the New Trade panel shows your bias chips at the top so your trades are checked against your own plan.

Step B — The New Trade panel

This is the heart of the cockpit — a slide-in panel on the right. Working top to bottom, you fill in:

1
Instrument: pair, setup, killzone (auto-detected from the current UTC time), and mode (live / sim / backtest).
2
Direction: long or short.
3
Prices: entry, stop loss and take profit — typed in by hand. Cairn instantly computes SL pips, TP pips, the RR ratio (colour-coded) and the lot size.
4
Risk calculator: enter risk as a $ amount or a % of the account; Cairn back-solves the lot size from your account balance, leverage, entry and SL.
5
Confluence: tick MSS confirmed, HTF bias aligned (auto-ticked if direction matches your session bias), DXY aligned, SMT confirmed (+ which correlated pair).
6
Invalidation: a free-text box — "this trade is wrong if…" — that requires at least 20 characters before you can submit. This is a deliberate honesty-forcing field.
7
Pre-trade emotional state: three 1–10 sliders — Calm, Urgency, and Need-to-win.
8
Rule check (live): as you fill the form, Cairn evaluates all your enabled rules in real time and lists each as a green tick, an amber warning, or a red block.
9
Optional: attach up to 4 chart screenshots.
10
Submit: "Save draft" (a planned trade you can activate later) or "Place order" → a confirmation modal asking you to confirm you've actually placed the order in your broker. The session then locks.
★ The defining feature

If a rule is set as a hard block (e.g. you've hit your daily loss limit, you're inside a cooldown after a loss, or you're outside a killzone), the "Place order" button is disabled and shakes — Cairn physically will not let you submit. Softer rules can be overridden, but only by typing an acknowledgement phrase and a reason, which is logged as a violation. This real-time prevention is what makes Cairn fundamentally different from a journal.

The 18 built-in rules the engine can enforce
  • Max risk per trade (%)
  • Max daily loss (%)
  • Max daily loss (fixed $)
  • Max overall daily loss — hard stop (%)
  • Minimum RR ratio
  • Position size matches plan
  • Require MSS confirmation
  • Require invalidation text
  • Require HTF bias logged
  • No stop-loss widening
  • Require killzone
  • Require DXY check
  • Max trades per day
  • Cooldown after a loss (minutes)
  • Daily stop after N losses
  • No revenge-trade window
  • Emotional-state gate
  • Weekend-holding blocked · Min trading days

Each is toggleable and configurable per account. Hard locks run first and short-circuit everything else.

1.5 — DURING: while the position is open

Because Cairn isn't connected to your broker (v1 is manual), the trade actually runs in MT5 / TradingView. Inside Cairn during this window you can:

  • See open positions and R-target alerts — a visual badge lights up as the trade reaches configurable R levels (e.g. 1R, 2R).
  • Log a partial close — open the close modal, switch to "partial", and record what % / how many lots you closed and at what price, leaving the rest running.
  • Stay under the circuit breakers — the session lock and daily-loss breaker remain active, so the next trade you try to log is still gated.

1.6 — AFTER: closing and reflecting

When the trade is done you open the Close Trade modal and complete it in sections:

EXIT DATA
  • Exit price (typed)
  • Exit time (datetime picker)
  • Exit reason (TP / SL / manual / BE / partial-then-full / timeout)
  • MAE & MFE pips (optional)
  • Live P&L / R / duration preview
HONESTY
  • Did you follow your plan exactly? (+ what changed)
  • Did you move your SL? (+ why)
  • Did you enter before MSS was confirmed?
  • Rules-broken checklist (self-reported)
REFLECTION
  • Post-trade calm score (slider)
  • What I did right (one line)
  • What I did wrong (one line)
  • Tags
EVIDENCE
  • Up to 4 screenshots (drag-drop or pick)
  • Each tagged: HTF / entry / exit / review

A trade with zero rules broken and the plan followed exactly is marked a clean trade; anything else is dirty. That clean/dirty flag is what drives your Discipline Ring and the behavioural analytics.

1.7 — What you get out of it

PREVENTION

Fewer impulsive, tilted and rule-breaking trades, because the worst ones are blocked before they happen.

SELF-KNOWLEDGE

Quantified answers: which setups, killzones and emotional states actually make you money — and which quietly bleed you.

A DISCIPLINE SCORE

A single number that goes up as you trade cleanly, turning "be more disciplined" into something measurable week over week.

02

Red Hat — Where the App Drags

Now the honest, adversarial read. You defined friction as time the app takes from a trader before, during and after a trade. By that definition Cairn is, today, a high-friction app — and some of that is by design (forcing self-awareness), but a lot of it is accidental and fixable. Here's where the time goes.

2.1 — The friction heat-map

Rough time cost of a single complete trade, captured end-to-end inside Cairn (not counting the actual broker execution):

Daily session bias 1×/day
2–4 min
New Trade panel (the big one)
60–120 s
Manual price entry (E/SL/TP)
re-typed
Confirmation modal ("it's placed?")
+1 click
Partial close logging
30–45 s
Close modal (exit + honesty + reflection)
90–180 s
The headline number

A single trade can cost a trader roughly 3–5 minutes of data entry inside Cairn, on top of the daily bias ritual — and almost all of it is typing things the trader already entered into their broker seconds earlier. Take 4 trades in a session and you're spending 15–25 minutes feeding the app.

2.2 — BEFORE-trade friction

⚠ The root cause: double entry

Cairn has no broker connection in v1. You set entry/SL/TP/lots in MT5 or TradingView, then re-type all of it into Cairn. Every number is entered twice, by hand, and can drift out of sync. This single architectural fact is the largest source of friction in the entire app — everything else is secondary.

  • The New Trade panel asks for ~15–20 inputs across instrument, direction, three prices, risk, four confluence checkboxes, invalidation text, and three emotion sliders — all in the heat of a killzone where price is moving and seconds matter.
  • The 20-character invalidation minimum is a hard gate. In the moment, typing two sentences of prose while a setup is forming is real pressure — and it's the field most likely to be rushed or gamed ("xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx").
  • Three emotion sliders, every single trade. Calm / Urgency / Need. Valuable data, but asking for three deliberate judgements per trade is heavy, and the defaults (7/3/3) mean a rushed trader just leaves them — quietly polluting the very behavioural dataset they exist to build.
  • Manual killzone is auto-detected (good), but pair/setup/confluence are not remembered between trades — a trader taking three EURUSD silver-bullet trades re-selects the same pair and setup three times.
  • Daily session bias requires six written reason fields (D/4H/1H bias + reason each). Thorough, but it's a 2–4 minute writing exercise before the first trade can even be planned.
  • The "Yes, it's placed" confirmation modal adds a second click to every order and relies entirely on the honour system — it confirms nothing real.

2.3 — DURING-trade friction

  • Cairn is blind to the live trade. Because there's no broker feed, R-target alerts can only fire off the planned numbers, and the app can't actually see price moving. The trader has to mentally bridge two screens.
  • Partial closes are a manual mini-form — switch mode, type lots, type price, type notes. Mid-trade, that's attention taken away from the chart.
  • SL moves / re-entries that happen on the broker are invisible until the trader self-reports them at close, by which point memory has faded.

2.4 — AFTER-trade friction

  • The close modal is a second large form — exit price, exit time, exit reason, MAE/MFE, three honesty yes/no questions (with conditional follow-ups), a rules-broken checklist, a calm slider, two reflection lines, tags and screenshots. That's another ~12–15 inputs.
  • Exit price and time are typed by hand — again, data the broker already has. The datetime picker for exit time is especially clumsy for something that should be "now".
  • The rules-broken checklist is self-reported — yet Cairn's own engine already knows which rules were at risk when the trade was placed. Asking the trader to re-declare violations duplicates work the app could infer.
  • MAE/MFE in pips requires the trader to go back to the chart and read off the maximum adverse/favourable excursion manually. Almost nobody will do this consistently by hand.
  • Nothing is batched. Every close is a full modal. There's no "I took five clean scalps, here they are" fast path — each is the full ceremony.

2.5 — Structural / hidden friction

  • Quick search / command palette is "coming soon" (it's literally labelled that in the top bar). For a keyboard-first pro tool, the absence of a fast jump-to-anything is felt.
  • The Review screen is a stub. The whole "turn data into weekly behaviour change" loop — the third of Cairn's three stated jobs — has no actual UI yet; only the PDF button works.
  • Reflection is demanded in the moment. Honesty fields are most truthful with a little distance, but Cairn front-loads them all into the close modal, when the trader most wants to walk away.
  • Two partial-close tables exist in the schema (one integer-encoded, one float) — a sign the partial-close flow grew twice. Minor for the user, but it's latent inconsistency that can surface as wrong numbers.
Important distinction — good friction vs bad friction

Good friction is the rule block, the override acknowledgement, the "this trade is wrong if…" prompt — these exist to slow down a bad action, and they should stay slow. Bad friction is everything that slows down a good action: re-typing prices the broker already knows, re-selecting the same pair, hand-entering exit times, self-reporting violations the engine could detect. Part 3 attacks the bad friction while protecting the good.

03

Thinking Hat — Cutting the Friction

The guiding principle from Cairn's own spec is exactly right: logging a good trade should be fast; breaking a rule should be slow. Today the app gets the second half right and the first half wrong. Here's how to fix the first half without weakening the second — sorted into what to automate, what to redesign, and the structural changes underneath.

3.1 — The one change that matters most: broker / platform integration

★ The 80/20 lever

An MT5 / cTrader / TradingView import collapses the largest friction source in the app. The data model already has the hooks for it (broker_source, broker_trade_id, imported_at), and v2 broker adapters are already on the roadmap. When fills flow in automatically, entry, SL, TP, lots, exit price, exit time, duration, partials and MAE/MFE all auto-populate. The trader stops being a data-entry clerk and Cairn becomes the cockpit the spec describes.

Concretely, integration lets Cairn move from "ask the trader" to "watch the trade":

Field Today (manual) With integration
Entry / SL / TP Typed by hand, twice Auto from order ticket
Lot size Calculated, then re-checked Auto from fill
Exit price & time Typed at close Auto on close event
Duration Derived from typed times Exact, automatic
Partial closes Manual mini-form each time Auto-logged as they happen
MAE / MFE Read off chart by hand Computed from tick data
SL moved? Self-reported from memory Detected — planned vs executed diff

3.2 — What else can be automated

  • Auto-detect rule violations instead of self-reporting them. The engine already evaluated every rule at entry. At close, compare planned vs actual and pre-tick the rules-broken checklist; let the trader confirm rather than recall. Honesty is preserved — the app proposes, the trader confirms or corrects.
  • Auto-flag SL-widening, early exit and revenge timing from execution data. These are objective facts once you have fills; they shouldn't be questions.
  • Derive confluence from the session. HTF-bias-aligned is already auto-ticked from the session bias — extend the same idea: if a session DXY bias is logged, pre-fill DXY-aligned; if the killzone is detected, the killzone field is done.
  • Screenshot OCR / paste. Let the trader paste a TradingView screenshot and have Cairn read entry/SL/TP off it (or at minimum attach instantly from clipboard rather than a file picker).
  • Auto P&L, R, %, duration, balance and Discipline-Ring update the moment a close lands — no recompute step, no stale dashboard.
  • Auto-rollover of yesterday's emotional baseline so the sliders start from your recent norm, not a fixed 7/3/3.

3.3 — Redesigns that cut clicks without losing the discipline

⚡ Setup templates / playbooks

Let a trader define a "Silver Bullet EURUSD" template that pre-fills pair, setup, killzone, required confluence and even default risk %. One tap loads the whole scaffold; the trader only types the three live prices. This is the single biggest pre-trade time saver after integration — and it's also how TradeZella's playbooks work.

⚡ Remember the last trade

Default the New Trade panel to the last pair/setup/mode used in this session. A trader scalping one pair shouldn't re-select it every time.

⚡ Invalidation quick-chips

Offer tappable ICT-native chips — "below the OB", "liquidity sweep fails", "closes back inside FVG" — that satisfy the requirement in one tap while still capturing structured, honest reasoning. Free text stays available for the unusual case.

⚡ One-tap emotional state

Replace three sliders with a single quick selector (Focused / Neutral / Tilted) that maps to sensible score triples, with the full sliders one tap away when the trader wants precision. Captures the signal, kills the ceremony.

⚡ "Clean trade" one-click close

If the plan was followed, a single "Closed clean at TP/SL" button fills the entire honesty section and most of the exit fields. Reserve the long form for trades that actually deviated — which are the ones worth reflecting on anyway.

⚡ Command palette + shortcuts

Ship the "coming soon" quick search as a ⌘K palette: new trade, close trade, jump to account, log bias. Power users live here; it turns a 4-click navigation into one keystroke.

3.4 — Structural change: split "the gate" from "the journal"

The big structural idea

Right now Cairn bundles two jobs into one heavy moment. Separate them:

Keep prevention instant; move reflection into a batched end-of-session queue.

This protects the discipline (the rule gate stays in the moment, where it must be) while moving the journaling weight to a single calm review at the end of the day — which is also more honest, because reflection benefits from distance. A "trades awaiting reflection" badge nudges the trader to clear the queue, and the Review screen (currently a stub) becomes the natural home for it.

04

Cairn vs TradeZella

TradeZella is the market-leading trade journal — cloud-based, ~$29–49/month, connected to 500+ brokers, built around analysing trades after the fact. Cairn is a local, private, discipline-first cockpit built around preventing bad trades in real time. They overlap in journaling and analytics but their centre of gravity is completely different.

4.1 — The fundamental difference

TRADEZELLA

"Reveal your edge"

A record-and-analyse tool. Trades flow in automatically after they happen; the value is in 50+ reports, replay and AI that tell you what worked. It improves you between sessions, by reflection.

CAIRN

"Build the stack"

A prevent-and-enforce tool. Rules are checked in real time and block bad trades before the click. It improves you during the session, by structure. Analytics is secondary to prevention.

The core insight

TradeZella explicitly does not prevent trades or enforce rules in real time — by their own positioning, it's a journal, not a brokerage or a gate. That is precisely the gap Cairn is built to fill. Cairn's real-time rule engine, hard locks and typed-acknowledgement overrides are something TradeZella structurally does not do.

4.3 — What Cairn has that TradeZella doesn't

PREVENTION

Real-time rule blocks, hard locks, cooldowns and circuit breakers that physically stop a bad trade. TZ can only tell you afterwards.

PSYCHOLOGY AS DATA

Structured calm/urgency/need scores feeding behavioural analytics — vs TZ's freeform notes with "no analytical layer on top".

ICT-NATIVE MODEL

Killzones, MSS, SMT, DXY, OB/FVG as first-class fields and rules — not just tags bolted onto a generic schema.

PRIVACY & OWNERSHIP

Local SQLite, no cloud, no subscription, no telemetry, no broker token expiry. Your data never leaves your machine.

4.4 — What TradeZella has that Cairn doesn't (yet)

AUTO IMPORT

500+ broker integrations with real-time, auto-tagged trade sync. Eliminates the double-entry that is Cairn's #1 friction.

TRADE REPLAY

Tick-by-tick chart replay with Level 2 and time & sales — review entries and exits exactly as they printed.

BACKTESTING

A real engine over 10+ years of data to validate a strategy before risking capital.

AI + BREADTH

Zella AI insights, 50+ reports, calendar view, mentor mode, and an education hub.

4.5 — What neither does well — Cairn's opening

  • Truly preventive, broker-aware discipline. If Cairn marries its rule engine to live broker data, it can do something TradeZella structurally won't: detect an SL being widened as it happens and warn, not just record it later. That's the genuine moat.
  • Psychology you can act on, not just log. Both apps capture emotion; neither turns it into in-the-moment intervention. Cairn already has the structured data to do "your win-rate drops 40% when urgency > 7 — sit this one out."
  • A real native mobile companion. Neither has one; a phone app that just shows the rule gate and the discipline score would be a differentiator.
  • Offline, owned, no-subscription pro tooling. For privacy-conscious or prop-firm traders wary of cloud journals, Cairn's local-first stance is a market position TradeZella can't copy without abandoning its model.
Strategic read

Don't try to out-feature TradeZella on reports, replay and education — that's their turf and a long chase. Instead, double down on the thing they can't do: real-time, broker-aware prevention. Add broker import primarily to feed the rule engine and kill friction, not to become another analytics dashboard. "The journal that stops the trade before you regret it" is a position TradeZella has explicitly ceded.
